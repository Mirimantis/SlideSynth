  **hard-quantised to the 12-EDO lattice**. This is the single biggest structural blocker: in
  19-EDO there is no way to pick "degree 7" as a root; you can only pick 100-cent multiples,
  which are not degrees of the tuning.
- `staff-renderer.ts` draws the **100-cent chromatic grid as the substrate** (line 48 loop
  steps by `CENTS_PER_SEMITONE`), labels it with `centsToNoteName`, and for microtonal scales
  draws *extra* dashed lines on top with labels of the form `C+50c` (lines 112–146).
  `highlightIntegers` is switched off whenever the scale is microtonal. So today the app's
  visual reference frame is always 12-EDO; a non-12 tuning is rendered as deviations from it.
- `snap.ts` chromatic fallback rounds to multiples of 100 cents; `hidePitchLines` (the shipped
  "None" mode, 8.19) suppresses both the lines and that fallback.
- `tuningOffsetCents` (8.27, `src/types.ts:131`) and `setReferenceA`/`centsToFreq` in
  `constants.ts` keep the A4 reference **entirely in the cents→Hz conversion**; `A4_CENTS = 6900`
  is a frozen anchor and the pitch grid never moves. This is already the correct separation
  (see §B) and should not be folded into the taxonomy.
- The flat catalog **already mixes two different axes** (see §F): `24tet` and `thai-7tet` are
  *tuning systems*; `dorian` and `blues` are *subsets of a tuning system*.

## A. Taxonomy: how tuning theorists actually categorise

### A.1 The standard split is regular vs. irregular, and rank is the real dimension

The mainstream music-theory split (Wikipedia, *Musical temperament*) is:

- **Just intonation** — untempered; every interval is a whole-number frequency ratio. **[SOURCE]**
- **Regular temperaments** — "generated with a finite number of generator intervals, most
  commonly a tempered perfect fifth, and a period, usually the octave." **[SOURCE]**
- **Irregular temperaments** — "cannot be described with such a generation process"; includes
  the well temperaments (Werckmeister, Kirnberger, Vallotti, Young), which have unequal fifths
  so that "some keys are more in tune than others, but all can be used." **[SOURCE]**
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_temperament>

The Xenharmonic Wiki formalises the regular side by **rank**: "A rank *r* regular temperament
may be defined by giving *r* multiplicatively independent real numbers… defined by *r*
generators and thus *r* vals." **[SOURCE]** So:

- **rank 1** = an equal temperament / EDO (one generator: the step),
- **rank 2** = a "linear" temperament — meantone, pythagorean, porcupine, superpyth… (period +
  generator),
- **rank n** → in the limit, JI itself is the untempered rank-∞ case. **[INFERENCE — the wiki
  frames RTs as approximations of "a JI subgroup", which is the same idea stated the other way]**
  <https://en.xen.wiki/w/Regular_temperament>, <https://en.xen.wiki/w/Tour_of_regular_temperaments>

"EDO" is a deliberate term of art: equal temperaments "are now often called equal divisions of
the octave (EDO)… in order to distinguish them from other equal tunings with the same number of
notes spanning an interval other than the octave" — i.e. EDO vs ED3 (Bohlen–Pierce) vs EDT.
**[SOURCE]** <https://en.xen.wiki/w/EDO>

### A.2 The user's top level conflates axes — concretely

**"TET/EDO | JI | Meantone | Other | None" is not a partition.** Three concrete overlaps:

1. **12-EDO *is* a meantone.** "12edo is a meantone temperament that tempers out 81/80 (the
   syntonic comma)… nearly indistinguishable from 1/11-comma meantone." **[SOURCE]**
   <https://en.xen.wiki/w/12edo>, <https://en.xen.wiki/w/Meantone_family>
2. **19-EDO and 31-EDO are also meantones** — both are in the user's proposed EDO list *and* in
   the meantone family (31-EDO ≈ 1/4-comma meantone). **[SOURCE — meantone family = rank-2
   temperaments tempering out 81/80; 19 and 31 both do]**
3. **Meantone is rank-2, EDO is rank-1** — they are points on the same axis (rank), not
   siblings. Choosing "meantone" leaves *which* meantone (1/4-comma, 1/6-comma, 12-EDO,
   19-EDO, 31-EDO) still open; choosing "19-EDO" already answers it. **[INFERENCE from the rank
   definitions above]**

So `EDO | Meantone` is a **rank-1 vs rank-2 distinction wearing a family costume**, and the two
sets intersect.

### A.3 The genuine category error at level 2 (JI)

The sketch's level-2 for JI lists *intervals*: `1:1, 9:8, 5:4, 3:2, 7:4, 2:1, 4:3`. This is a
category error in the way the task description already suspects:

- `1:1` and `2:1` are not choices at all — they are the unison and the equave, present in every
  octave-repeating JI scale.
- The other entries are **single intervals**, not systems. Picking "3:2" does not define a
  pitch set the staff can draw.
- JI systems are conventionally selected by **prime limit** ("a pitch system whose ratios
  contain the prime number n and no higher primes is an n-prime-limit system"; 5-limit = ratios
  built from 2, 3, 5) or **odd limit** (used for tonality diamonds: odd part of numerator and
  denominator ≤ n), or by naming a **specific constructed scale** — a tonality diamond, a
  combination product set, a Euler–Fokker genus, or a historical scale like Partch's 43-tone
  (built on the 11-limit tonality diamond). **[SOURCE]**
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_(music)>, <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonality_diamond>,
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Partch%27s_43-tone_scale>

There is a second, subtler error: **level 2 is labelled "Interval/size" as if it were one
parameter**, but the parameter is different in kind per family — *N* (a count) for EDO, *n* (a
limit) for JI, a *comma or generator* for a rank-2 temperament, and *nothing* (a named table)
for a well temperament. That's fine for a UI that swaps the whole control per family; it is not
fine if level 2 is conceived as "one dropdown whose options change."

### A.4 What practitioners find least confusing

I found **no usability study or survey** on this. **[INFERENCE, from what shipping tools
actually do — see §E]**: the least-confusing top level in practice is *provenance/purpose*
rather than *mathematical rank*, because rank is invisible to the user. The de facto grouping
tools use is roughly:

`Equal divisions` · `Just intonation` · `Historical / well temperaments` · `World & traditional`
· `Custom / imported`

with "meantone" living inside *Historical* (as 1/4-comma meantone, a named table) and *also*
reachable as 19-EDO/31-EDO inside *Equal divisions*. Duplicated entry points, no claimed
partition. That duplication is honest — the sets genuinely overlap — and it's what Pianoteq,
Ableton and Surge all effectively do.

## B. The orthogonal axes

The five axes named in the brief map almost exactly onto the **Scala file-format split**, which
is the strongest evidence they are real and separable.

`.scl` "specifies the pitch intervals of a musical scale… The first note of 1/1 or 0.0 cents is
implicit and not in the files," and it explicitly does **not** encode root note, reference
frequency, or key mapping. That's what `.kbm` (keyboard mapping) is for, and Scala keeps a
separate base frequency, with `COPY/FREQ_MAP` and `COPY/MAP_FREQ` to move values between them.
**[SOURCE]** <https://www.huygens-fokker.org/scala/scl_format.html>,
<https://www.huygens-fokker.org/scala/help.htm>

Surge states the division plainly: SCL "describe[s] the relationships between the notes in a
scale", KBM "determine[s] how those relations are laid out across the MIDI notes", and "you can
apply the same scale in different keys by swapping KBM files." **[SOURCE]**
<https://surge-synthesizer.github.io/tuning-guide/>

| Axis | Depends on | Free? |
|---|---|---|
| (i) tuning / temperament — the pitch-class universe | nothing | **free** (root of the cascade) |
| (ii) reference frequency (Hz anchor; SlideSynth's Tune A4) | nothing musically | **free** |
| (iii) root / 1-1 — which degree is "home" | (i): legal roots are *degrees of the tuning* | **cascades** |
| (iv) scale/mode subset | (i) for membership; (iii) for rotation | **cascades from both** |
| (v) note naming / keyboard mapping | (i) | **cascades from (i); independent of (iii)/(iv)** |

Notes and caveats:

- **(ii) is genuinely orthogonal, and SlideSynth already has it right.** `tuningOffsetCents` is
  applied in `centsToFreq` only; the grid never moves. In Scala/Ableton terms this is the
  "reference frequency" half of the KBM, decoupled from which *degree* it anchors.
- **(ii) and (iii) are entangled in the file formats but not in SlideSynth.** ASCL's
  `@ABL REFERENCE_PITCH <octave> <note-index> <freq>` binds a *degree index* to a *frequency*
  in one directive — so in Ableton the reference pitch is a (degree, Hz) pair. **[SOURCE]**
  <https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/10998372840220-ASCL-Specification>. Live's UI
  exposes exactly this as three controls: **Octave**, **Note**, **Ref. Pitch/Freq**. **[SOURCE]**
  <https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-tuning-systems/>. SlideSynth's frozen
  `A4_CENTS = 6900` anchor sidesteps that entanglement; **[INFERENCE]** keeping them separate is
  the simpler model and there's no reason to import the coupling.
- **(iii) root ≠ 1/1.** Leimma's guide is explicit: a reference pitch "will be used as the 1:1
  ratio or 0 cents division in the tuning," and "the 1:1 division does not have to be the root
  of your subset." **[SOURCE]** — i.e. there are potentially *two* root-ish concepts: the
  tuning's 1/1, and the mode's tonic. SlideSynth currently has only one (`scaleRoot`).
  <https://www.open-access.bcu.ac.uk/14036/13/App%205.%20Allami,%20K.%20(2021a)%20-%20Apotome%20and%20Leimma%20user%20guide.pdf>
- **(v) is the axis SlideSynth doesn't have at all.** ASCL added `@ABL NOTE_NAMES` precisely
  because `.scl` under-specifies naming. **[SOURCE]** `staff-renderer.ts` hard-codes
  `centsToNoteName` against the 12-EDO grid — in 19-EDO those labels are wrong, not merely
  approximate.

**Consequence for the number of dropdowns:** (ii) is a separate always-visible control (already
is). (v) should be derived from (i), not chosen. That leaves **three** user-facing choices —
tuning, root, subset — not four.

## C. Naming and notation per family — how level 3 degrades

### 12-EDO and other meantone EDOs
"C major" is meaningful in **19-EDO**: "Conventional notation works perfectly with 19edo as long
as you remember that C♯ and D♭ are different notes." **[SOURCE]**
<https://en.xen.wiki/w/19edo>. This works because 19-EDO is a meantone, so the chain of fifths
generates the familiar 7 letter names + sharps/flats, and the 5L2s diatonic scale exists.

### Non-meantone EDOs
**22-EDO** breaks: "7 fifths reduces to three EDOsteps, and the usual chain of fifths creates
the scale C – D♭ – B♯ – C♯ – D – E♭ – F♭ – D♯ – E – F… B♯–D♭ looks ascending on the page but
sounds descending." 22-EDO therefore "require[s] ups and downs to notate all intervals."
**[SOURCE]** <https://en.xen.wiki/w/22edo>, <https://en.xen.wiki/w/Ups_and_downs_notation>.
Kite's ups-and-downs adds `^`/`v` accidentals each altering by one edostep.

### What tools actually display
- **Scale Workshop** uses **degree/EDO notation `n\m`** (e.g. `7\12` = 7 steps of 12-EDO) as a
  first-class input form alongside ratios, cents, monzos and FJS. **[SOURCE]**
  <https://github.com/xenharmonic-devs/scale-workshop>
- **Surge** names equal divisions **`ED<interval>-<divisions>`**: `ED2-12` = 12-EDO,
  `ED3-13` = Bohlen–Pierce. **[SOURCE]** <https://surge-synthesizer.github.io/tuning-guide/>
- **Ableton** ships per-tuning custom names via `@ABL NOTE_NAMES`, defaulting to
  `C C♯/D♭ D …` for 12. **[SOURCE]**
- **Leimma** works from a chosen reference pitch and a circular/ratio display rather than letter
  names. **[SOURCE, partial — I could not extract the full PDF text, see §Limitations]**

### JI
"Key" is replaced by the **1/1**: a pitch plus a frequency. Leimma: "To create, hear and play
any tuning system, a reference pitch must be chosen to tune all the different divisions in
relation to it. This reference pitch, or its octave equivalents, will be used as the 1:1 ratio
or 0 cents division." **[SOURCE]**

### Degradation ladder for level 3 **[INFERENCE — this synthesis is mine]**
1. 12-EDO / historical 12-note temperaments → **letter names** (C, C♯…), and the root *matters
   sonically* for irregular temperaments because keys are unequal.
2. Meantone EDOs (19, 31) → **letter names with distinct ♯/♭**.
3. Other EDOs (5, 7, 9, 22, 53) → **degree index `0..N-1`** (or `n\N`), optionally
   letter+ups/downs.
4. JI / imported .scl → **degree index + ratio label**, root labelled "1/1".
5. None → control hidden.

## D. Modes/scales per family — enumerate or generate?

### 12-EDO — enumeration is correct and small
The familiar 7 modes + harmonic/melodic minor + pentatonics + blues + whole-tone + octatonic +
a world-scale tail. This is ~20–30 entries. **The existing catalog is already exactly this and
is fine.**

### Non-12 EDOs — generation, not enumeration
The conventional vocabulary is **MOS (moment of symmetry) scales**, invented by Erv Wilson in
1975. A MOS is specified by a **period, a generator, and a step signature**: "Every MOS can be
uniquely specified by giving its signature, i.e. the number of large and small steps… notated
e.g. `5L 2s`." The diatonic scale *is* `5L 2s` (LLsLLLs for the major mode), and "in the context
of moment-of-symmetry scales, the term diatonic exclusively refers to 5L 2s." **[SOURCE]**
<https://en.xen.wiki/w/MOS_scale>, <https://sethares.engr.wisc.edu/software/TFSdocs/aboutMOS.html>

The Xenharmonic Wiki's **TAMNAMS** scheme supplies human names for MOS signatures. **[SOURCE]**
<https://en.xen.wiki/w/TAMNAMS/Appendix>

There is a **maintained MIT-licensed JS library** for this:
`xenharmonic-devs/moment-of-symmetry` — `mos(numberOfLargeSteps, numberOfSmallSteps, opts)`
with `sizeOfLargeStep` / `sizeOfSmallStep` / `down` (mode brightness), plus `mosModes()` and
`mosPatterns()`; its own docs demonstrate generating "a subset of 31edo" by giving step sizes in
edosteps. **[SOURCE]** <https://github.com/xenharmonic-devs/moment-of-symmetry>

**So: a fixed enumerated list is the wrong UI for non-12 EDOs.** The number of MOS scales per
EDO is large and grows with N, and the natural control is *(L, s, mode-rotation)* — three
integers — not a list. **[INFERENCE, but directly supported by the fact that Scale Workshop's
UI for this is a generator dialog, not a list — see §E]**

### JI — construction, not modes
The vocabulary is **prime limit / odd limit / tonality diamond / combination product set /
Euler–Fokker genus / named historical scales (Partch 43)**. **[SOURCE]** These are
*constructors with parameters*, matching Scale Workshop's menu exactly.

### Irregular / well temperaments — the whole system is the scale
Werckmeister III, Kirnberger III, Vallotti, Young, 1/4-comma meantone are each a fixed 12-note
table. "Mode" is then just the ordinary 12-EDO mode list applied on top — but **the root is
sonically load-bearing** here in a way it is not for any EDO, since the keys are deliberately
unequal ("some keys are more in tune than others"). **[SOURCE]** This family is the strongest
argument for keeping root as a first-class, always-visible control.

## E. Existing UI patterns

### The archive: flat, huge, text-searched
The Scala scale archive is "over 5,000 Scala scale files"; its index is a plain text listing,
`scalesdir.txt`. **[SOURCE]** <https://www.huygens-fokker.org/scala/downloads.html>,
<https://en.xen.wiki/w/Scala_scale_archive>. No hierarchy — a flat directory plus descriptions.

### Ableton Live 12 — flat browser list + .scl drag-drop + separate reference controls
Tunings live in the browser under a "Tunings" label, Core Library plus user files, "sorted
numerically and alphabetically." Any `.scl` can be loaded by dragging it into the Tuning section.
Reference pitch is three separate controls (Octave / Note / Ref. Pitch/Freq) plus Lowest/Highest
Note. **[SOURCE]** <https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-tuning-systems/>,
<https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/11535414344476-Tuning-Systems-FAQ>.
**Ableton — with a fresh 2024 design and heavy pedagogical investment (tuning.ableton.com) —
did not build a cascade.** They built a searchable list + import + an explanatory website.

### Surge XT — menu + drag-drop + a 5-tab editor
`.scl`/`.kbm` load from a Factory Tuning Library, drag-and-drop anywhere on the UI, or the
built-in Tuning Editor, whose tabs are **Scala / Polar / Interval / To Equal / Rotation** (plus
"True Keys"). Equal divisions are named `ED2-12`, `ED3-13`. Surge can also act as an MTS-ESP
source. **[SOURCE]** <https://surge-synthesizer.github.io/tuning-guide/>

### ODDSOUND MTS-ESP Master — scale list + parameterised generators + import
"Import tunings and mappings in .scl, .kbm and .tun formats, or create your own tuning system,
either manually or algorithmically. Scale 'types' are available for creating scales defined by
parameters." Plus a Graph page where "intervals [can be] snapped to harmonics, steps of another
scale or an integer number of cents." **[SOURCE]** <https://oddsound.com/>,
<https://oddsound.com/dl/ODDSound_MTS_ESP_Suite_Master_Manual.pdf>
(*I could not extract the PDF's full scale-type list — see Limitations.*)

### Scale Workshop — a menu of **constructors**, not families
The "New" menu offers: **Equal temperaments** ("Number of divisions", "Interval to divide"),
**Rank-2 temperaments** ("using a generator and period"), **Harmonic series segment** (lowest &
highest harmonic), **Subharmonic series segment**, **Enumerate chord** (colon-separated
harmonics), **Combination product set**. Plus a free-text "Scale data" field; SW3 replaces it
with the SonicWeave DSL and adds MOS pattern display. **[SOURCE]**
<https://seanarchibald.github.io/scale-workshop/guide.htm>,
<https://github.com/xenharmonic-devs/scale-workshop>, <https://sevish.com/2022/scale-workshop-2-tutorials/>

This is the single most instructive precedent: **the top-level menu is a list of *construction
methods with parameters*, not a taxonomy of families.** "Equal temperaments" and "Rank-2
temperaments" sit side by side without any claim that they're disjoint.

### Entonal Studio — preset browser + graphical editing
"Over a hundred tunings in the preset browser, no knowledge of microtonal theory is needed";
intervals expressible "using ratios, cents, or mathematical expressions"; a Radial Graph and a
Lattice view; can generate equal temperaments; up to 192 notes. **[SOURCE]**
<https://node.audio/products/entonal-studio>, <https://www.kvraudio.com/product/entonal-studio-by-node-audio>

### Leimma — the one real cascade
Homepage offers **[Select a Tuning System]** (loads a database of existing tunings) or
**[Create a Tuning System]**; then a **reference frequency** must be chosen ("used as the 1:1
ratio or 0 cents division"); then a **subset** (scale/mode) is drawn from the tuning, with its
own root that "does not have to be the 1:1". **[SOURCE]** — this is exactly
`tuning → reference → subset → root`, i.e. the user's sketch done correctly.
<https://www.open-access.bcu.ac.uk/14036/13/App%205.%20Allami,%20K.%20(2021a)%20-%20Apotome%20and%20Leimma%20user%20guide.pdf>
Note Leimma's purpose is explicitly pedagogical/decolonial exploration, not fast production
work. **[SOURCE]** <https://sevish.com/2021/leimma-apotome-two-new-web-tools-for-microtonal-music/>

### Pianoteq — flat grouped preset menu + key
A menu of named historical temperaments (Equal, meantone, Kellner, Kirnberger III, Werckmeister,
just), grouped by type, with a key/root selector — because unequal temperaments need one.
**[SOURCE, weak — forum posts and product descriptions rather than the manual]**
<https://forum.modartt.com/viewtopic.php?id=4116>

### Does anyone do the cascade? Does everyone converge on .scl?
- **Cascade:** only **Leimma**, and it is an exploration tool. **[SOURCE]**
- **.scl import:** effectively universal — Ableton, Surge, MTS-ESP, Entonal, Scale Workshop,
  Pianoteq all read `.scl`. **[SOURCE for the first five]**
- **The converged shape is three-part:** (1) a browsable/searchable named-preset list,
  (2) a small set of parameter-driven constructors for the generative families,
  (3) `.scl` import as the escape hatch — with reference pitch as a separate always-visible
  control. **[INFERENCE, but every tool in §E fits this shape.]**

### Reported usability complaints
**I found no systematic usability study, survey, or well-sourced complaint corpus.** The only
signal I can cite is structural: `.scl` alone under-specifies, which is why Ableton had to
invent `.ascl` (adding `REFERENCE_PITCH` and `NOTE_NAMES`) and why Scala needs `.kbm` at all.
**[SOURCE]** Anything stronger about "what users find confusing" would be me making it up.

## F. Recommendation — three concrete shapes

### Diagnosis first
The flat `SCALE_CATALOG`'s real defect is **not missing hierarchy — it's that one control is
serving two orthogonal axes**:

| Existing entry | Actually is |
|---|---|
| major, minor, harmonic/melodic minor, dorian…locrian, pentatonics, blues, whole tone, chromatic, dim HW/WH, hungarian minor, double harmonic, hirajoshi, in-sen, bhairav | **Axis B** — subsets of 12-EDO. Clean. |
| `24tet` | **Axis A** — an EDO masquerading as a scale (it's the *chromatic* of 24-EDO). |
| `thai-7tet` | **Axis A** — 7-EDO. (Also proves non-12 EDOs already work in the data model.) |
| `maqam-rast`, `maqam-bayati` | **A+B fused** — subsets of 24-EDO, presented as if they were 12-EDO scales. |
| `slendro`, `pelog` | **Axis A**, and *irregular* — neither EDO nor JI. The shipped values are one arbitrary rendering of a family of instrument-specific tunings. |
| `chromatic` | Axis A's identity element appearing in Axis B. |

Every shape below is evaluated against that table.

---

### Shape 1 — the strict cascade, corrected

Four dependent controls:

1. **Family** — `Equal divisions (EDO/EDn)` · `Just intonation` · `Regular temperament (rank-2)`
   · `Historical & irregular` · `Traditional / instrument tunings` · `None`
   *(fixes the sketch: meantone moves inside "Regular temperament" and "Historical"; 12-EDO
   appears under both Equal divisions and, honestly, is also a meantone — accept the overlap
   rather than pretending it's a partition.)*
2. **System parameter** — control type swaps per family:
   EDO → `N` (5, 7, 9, 12, 19, 22, 24, 31, 53 …) + optional equave (2/1, 3/1);
   JI → prime limit (3, 5, 7, 11, 13) or a named construction (tonality diamond, CPS, Partch 43);
   Rank-2 → named temperament (1/4-comma meantone, Pythagorean, superpyth, porcupine) or
   raw generator in cents;
   Historical → named table (Werckmeister III, Kirnberger III, Vallotti, Young);
   Traditional → named tuning (Slendro, Pelog, …).
   ***This is where the sketch's "1:1, 9:8, 3:2" list must be replaced — those are intervals,
   not systems.***
3. **Root / 1-1** — degrees of the chosen system: letter names for 12/19, `0..N-1` for other
   EDOs, ratio labels for JI.
4. **Scale / mode** — enumerated list for 12-EDO and historical; **MOS generator control
   (L, s, mode rotation)** for other EDOs; subset picker for JI.

**Pros:** teachable; matches Leimma, the one shipping cascade; makes the axis confusion in the
current catalog impossible to reintroduce; each level has a defensible theoretical basis.
**Cons:** four controls to reach "C major", which is >90% of use **[INFERENCE]**; dependent
dropdowns reset badly when the user changes level 1 (classic cascade complaint); "Traditional /
instrument tunings" is a junk drawer; maqam entries have no honest home (§C of the table above);
level-4 for non-12 EDOs is a *different kind of widget* from level-4 for 12-EDO, which
undermines the "four dropdowns" framing.

---

### Shape 2 — two axes + root (**my recommendation for the core model**)

Three always-visible controls:

- **Tuning** (Axis A — the pitch-class universe): `12-EDO` (default) · other EDOs · JI limit sets
  · historical tables · traditional tunings · `Imported (.scl)` · `None`.
  Grouped/searchable, with parameter fields revealed only for the generative entries
  (`Equal divisions: N = __, equave = __`).
- **Root** (which degree is home) — options generated by Axis A.
- **Scale** (Axis B — subset of Axis A): filtered to the current tuning; for 12-EDO this is
  literally today's catalog minus the four Axis-A strays; for other EDOs it offers
  `Chromatic (all degrees)` + a MOS generator; for JI it offers `All` + named subsets.

**Pros:** it is the *minimum honest* fix — it names the exact defect in the current catalog and
nothing more; it collapses the sketch's levels 1+2 into one control, which is right because
"family" and "which member of the family" are one choice from the user's point of view (you pick
"19-EDO", not "EDO" then "19"); the 12-EDO default path is unchanged from today, so nothing
regresses for existing users; it matches how Ableton/Surge/Entonal present things (one tuning
picker + separate reference controls).
**Cons:** the Tuning picker still has to be grouped/searchable internally, so you haven't
escaped taxonomy — you've just hidden it inside one control; discoverability of *why* 19-EDO and
31-EDO are related (both meantones) is lost.
**Catalog mapping:** clean for every row of the table. `24tet`/`thai-7tet` become Axis A
entries; maqam scales become Axis B subsets *of* 24-EDO (honest, and it makes `maqam-rast`'s
`[0, 2, 3.5, 5, 7, 9, 10.5]` legible as 24-EDO degrees `[0,4,7,10,14,18,21]`); slendro/pelog
become Axis A irregular tunings with no subsets (or Pelog + patet as subsets).

### Shape 3 — search-first list + constructors + import (**the industry-converged answer**)

- One **searchable, tag-faceted "Tuning" field** — type "19", "meantone", "pelog", "dorian" and
  get ranked results. Facet chips: `12-EDO` `EDO` `JI` `Historical` `World` `Non-octave` `User`.
- A small **"New…" menu of constructors** mirroring Scale Workshop: *Equal divisions* (N,
  equave), *MOS* (L, s, mode), *Harmonic segment* (lo, hi), *Rank-2* (generator, period).
- **`.scl` import** as a first-class path.
- **Root** and **Tune A4** as separate always-visible controls (already true for A4).
- Scale/mode remains a second control when the selected tuning has enumerated subsets.

**Pros:** this is what every shipping tool converged on (§E); scales to thousands of entries
without UI growth; matches how people actually retrieve a tuning (by name, not by derivation);
constructors handle the generative families without pretending to enumerate them.
**Cons:** a search box teaches nothing — SlideSynth's staff-line rendering makes it a plausible
*learning* tool, and search-first throws that away; needs decent fuzzy search + tag curation;
harder to make discoverable on a small toolbar.

### Cross-cutting: should `.scl` import be a hedge? **Yes.**

Argument for: it caps the built-in taxonomy at a curated ~15–20 entries permanently; the 5000+
file archive already exists and is freely redistributable **[SOURCE]**; every peer tool accepts
it, so it's the expected affordance; and `.scl` maps almost 1:1 onto `ScaleDefinition` — cents
or ratio lines become `intervals`, the final line becomes `period`, both fields already float.

Caveats to raise in the design session:
- `.scl` supplies **no root, no reference frequency, no note names** **[SOURCE]** — so import
  does *not* remove the need to decide §C's naming ladder, and Ableton's `.ascl` extension is
  the precedent for adding those as metadata.
- `.scl` **conflates Axis A and Axis B**: a file called "D Dorian in 5-limit JI" is just a list
  of intervals. Imported scales therefore land in a hybrid slot in Shape 2. **[INFERENCE]**
- Files can be **non-octave** (`ED3-13`) or **very large** (Partch 43, MTS-ESP allows 192, and
  Entonal 192 **[SOURCE]**). `getScaleNotes` builds *every* note across the whole staff range
  and caches per `(scaleId, root)`; a 192-note scale over the full pitch range is a large array,
  and `staff-renderer` draws a line per note. Worth a perf sanity check. **[INFERENCE from
  reading the code]**
- Import means user-supplied file content reaching the renderer — the description line is
  free text and should be treated as untrusted display data.

### Data-model consequences shared by all three shapes (not an implementation plan)

1. **`scaleRoot: 0..11` must generalise** — to a cents offset or a degree index into Axis A.
   Without this, no shape works for non-12 tunings; you'd have 12 roots at 100-cent spacing
   inside a 19-tone system.
2. **`staff-renderer`'s 100-cent substrate becomes a per-tuning decision.** Today non-12 scales
   render as dashed deviations from the 12-EDO grid with `C+50c` labels — defensible as a
   *transitional* view, arguably wrong as the permanent one for a 19-EDO piece. The `hidePitchLines`
   plumbing already proves the substrate can be switched off.
3. **`centsToNoteName` needs a naming strategy per tuning** — the `@ABL NOTE_NAMES` idea.
4. **Tune A4 stays where it is.** It is already the correct, orthogonal reference-frequency axis.
5. **"None" is not a tuning family** — it's a display/snap mode (no grid, no chromatic fallback).
   Keeping it as a sentinel in the Tuning control is pragmatic and already ships; just don't let
   the taxonomy pretend it's a peer of "JI".
6. **`period` as a float already supports non-octave equaves** for free — a cheap, real
   capability (Bohlen–Pierce = ED3-13) that no shape has to design around.

## Open questions for the human planning session

1. **How many always-visible controls is the toolbar willing to spend?** Shape 2 needs three
   (Tuning / Root / Scale); today there are two. This is the decision everything else hangs off.
2. **Does the staff keep the 12-EDO chromatic substrate under non-12 tunings, or switch to the
   tuning's own degrees?** (Affects `staff-renderer`, label format, and whether the app is a
   12-EDO tool with microtonal decoration or a genuinely tuning-agnostic one.)
3. **Root representation: cents offset, or degree index into the tuning?** Degree index is more
   correct and survives tuning changes badly; cents offset is closer to today's model.
4. **Two roots or one?** Leimma distinguishes the tuning's 1/1 from the mode's tonic. Is that
   distinction worth a control here, or collapse to one?
5. **MOS generator control for non-12 EDOs — in scope, or defer?** It's the theoretically right
   answer for §D, it has a ready MIT library, and it's also a whole second UI.
6. **`.scl` import — in the first cut or a later hedge?** If later, the taxonomy must be sized
   to stand alone in the meantime.
7. **Where do maqam, slendro and pelog honestly live**, given the shipped values are
   quantised/arbitrary approximations? Reclassify under 24-EDO / irregular, relabel as
   "approximation", or leave as-is with a note?
8. **Do overlapping entry points (19-EDO reachable as both "EDO" and "meantone") get shipped, or
   is a clean partition worth the theoretical dishonesty?**
9. **Note naming per tuning** — degree numbers everywhere for non-12, or letter names where the
   tuning is meantone-compatible (12, 19, 31)?
10. **Migration:** existing saved compositions carry `scaleId` + `scaleRoot: 0..11`. What do
    `24tet`, `thai-7tet`, `maqam-*`, `slendro`, `pelog` map to under the new model, and does the
    golden-format test need a compatibility shim?
